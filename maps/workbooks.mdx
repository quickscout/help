A workbook is a set of sheets, like slides, that makes a point: where an operator drills, how two frac companies compare, or the wells you plan to drill. Each sheet holds maps, production charts, tables, figures and your own words.

Making, changing and sharing workbooks is part of the [paid plans](/get-started/plans). Reading one isn't: anyone you share a workbook with can read it, present it and print it, and a workbook shared by link opens for anyone with the link, without signing in. See [Present, print & share a workbook](/maps/workbooks-share).

<Note>
  A workbook keeps the data it was made with. Each block of data shows its month, such as *Data as of Aug 2026*, and its numbers stay the same until you refresh them. See [Keep the data current](#keep-the-data-current).
</Note>

## Your pages

**Your pages** lists your workbooks, the ones shared with you, and the pages Ask has [published for you](/maps/pages). To get there, click **Your pages** at the top left of any workbook, or go to [quickscout.io/workbooks](https://quickscout.io/workbooks). You need to be signed in.

{/* screenshot: workbooks-library — Your pages with the All, Pages, Workbooks and Templates chips and their counts, Search your pages, the Everything and Last edited menus, and rows for a Help demo workbook, a template and a page. Annotate: 1 = the kind chips, 2 = New workbook */}

- **All**, **Pages**, **Workbooks** and **Templates** narrow the list. Each shows its count.
- **Search your pages** finds a title.
- The first menu shows **Everything**, only **Yours**, only what's **Shared with you**, or the **Trash**. The second sorts by **Last edited**, **Created** or **Title**.
- Each row shows its kind, its title and number of sheets, its project, who can see it, and when it was last edited. Click the title to open it.
- **More** on a row has **Open in a new tab**, **Copy link** and, for your own, **Move to trash**.
- In the **Trash**, **Restore** brings a workbook back with all its versions.
- **New workbook** starts one, and **Back to Maps** returns to the map.

## Start a workbook

Click **New workbook** on Your pages, or **New workbook…** in the **Add to workbook** card in Maps. The dialog has three parts: **1 · Start from**, **2 · Fill in** and **3 · Name it**.

### Choose a start

| Start | Sheets | What it shows | Asks for |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Operator review** | 8 | Where an operator drills, what it produces, and its top leases | An operator, an area and a month range |
| **Area overview** | 6 | Operators, wells and production in a county | A county and a month range |
| **Drilling activity** | 5 | Permits, spuds and completions over a month range | A county and a month range |
| **Service-company comparison** | 5 | Two frac companies' jobs side by side, each in its own colour | Two frac companies, an area and a month range |
| **Drilling program** | 5 | Planned wells on a map, the schedule built from them, and offset production | An operator and an area |
| **Blank** | 1 | One empty sheet, to build by hand | Nothing |

Under **Your templates**, the dialog also lists the templates you've saved, and any shared with you. See [Reuse a workbook](#reuse-a-workbook).

When you start from the **Add to workbook** card, the dialog opens on **Blank**. Whichever start you choose, the thing you were adding goes in as a new sheet.

### Fill it in

- **Operator**: type two letters of the name, or its P-5 number, and pick it from the operators as filed with the RRC.
- **County**: type the county's name.
- **First frac company** and **Second frac company**: pick each from the frac companies on file.
- **Area**: *State of Texas*, or a basin such as the *Permian Basin*.
- **Month range**: *Last 12 months*, *Last 24 months*, *Last 5 years*, *Since Jan 2019* or *Year to date*. These count back from the latest month of production on file, so they move on when you refresh the data later.

You can change any of these later, on the workbook's **Params** tab.

### Name it

**Title** fills itself in from what you picked, until you type your own. The line under it lists the sheets you'll get.

{/* enterprise-hold: a sentence after the Title sentence above.
**Project** files the workbook under one of your [projects](/enterprise/projects).
*/}

**Create workbook** stays grey until everything is filled in. Hold the pointer over it to see what's missing, such as *Pick an operator first.*

From Your pages, the new workbook then opens and captures its data, which takes about 30 seconds. From the **Add to workbook** card, you stay on the map, and a row at the bottom of it offers **Open workbook**.

## The editor

A workbook you can change opens in the editor.

{/* screenshot: workbooks-editor — The editor on sheet 3 of the Help demo operator review, a Map + side sheet, with no block selected and the Sheet tab open. Annotate: 1 = Sheets on the left, 2 = Add block under the sheet, 3 = the Block, Sheet, Params and Notes tabs, 4 = the footer, 5 = Versions, Present and Share */}

- At the top: **Your pages**, the title (click it to rename the workbook), who can see it (click to share), and whether your last change is saved, such as *Saved · just now*. On the right are **Ask**, **Versions**, **Present**, **Share** and **More**.
- On the left: **Sheets**.
- In the middle: the sheet, with a line under it such as *Sheet 3 of 9 · Map + side · 3 blocks*, and **Add block**.
- On the right: a panel with four tabs: **Block**, **Sheet**, **Params** and **Notes**.
- At the bottom: a footer that tells you about the data, and anything that needs you.

Changes save by themselves a moment after you stop. If you try to leave before a change is saved, your browser asks first.

**More** has **Rename**, **Copy link**, **Make a copy**, **Print**, **Undo**, **Redo** and, for your own workbook, **Move to trash**.

### Sheets

Click a sheet on the left to open it. Drag a sheet up or down to change the order.

The open sheet has three buttons:

- **Duplicate** adds a copy right after it.
- **Hide** keeps the sheet for editors only. It's marked *Hidden from viewers*, and Present, printing and the people you share with skip it. **Show** brings it back.
- **More** has **Move up**, **Move down** and **Delete sheet**.

**Add sheet** asks for a layout:

| Layout | Holds |
|---|---|
| **Cover** | A title page. On the **Sheet** tab, fill in its title, **Subtitle**, **Kicker (above the title)**, **Prepared for** and **Prepared by**. A map can fill its right side. |
| **Map** | A map that fills the sheet, with up to three blocks floating at its top right. |
| **Map + side** | A map on the left, and a column of up to three blocks on the right. |
| **Grid** | Blocks on a 12 × 6 grid. Drag a block's tab to move it, and its corner to resize it. |
| **Text** | Your words, with one block beside them. |

The **Sheet** tab also changes the open sheet's **Layout** (a layout its blocks won't fit is greyed out), its **Sheet title**, and **Hide this sheet**. **Blocks on this sheet** lists its blocks: click one to select it.

### Blocks

Click **Add block**, under the sheet or on the **Sheet** tab, and pick a kind. The block goes in the sheet's next free place. A kind that can't go on this sheet yet is greyed out; hold the pointer over it to see why.

| Block | Shows | Needs first |
|---|---|---|
| **Text** | Your words, with headings, bold and lists | Nothing |
| **Map** | A map drawn like a printed one. See [Maps on a sheet](#maps-on-a-sheet). | Nothing |
| **Production chart** | Oil, gas and water by month, on log paper | An operator, lease, field, county or well on the **Params** tab |
| **Stat** | One figure in a sentence, with a small chart | The same |
| **Table** | Rows of leases or wells: the top 10 at first, and a button to see them all | The same |
| **Scorecard**, **Radar chart**, **Distribution**, **Type curve** | Groups of frac jobs compared | A group of frac jobs. See [Groups](#groups). |
| **Cohort curves** | Production by month on production, for each group, or a record's wells | A group, or a record on the **Params** tab |
| **Drilling schedule** | The planned wells on the workbook's maps, by spud. See [Planned wells and the schedule](#planned-wells-and-the-schedule). | Nothing |

Two more kinds aren't in **Add block**: a comparison chart, which you add from the compare tray, and an Ask answer, pinned with the question it answered and marked *From Ask*. See [Add to a workbook from Maps](#add-to-a-workbook-from-maps).

A sheet holds up to 12 blocks, two of them maps.

### Change a block

Click a block on the sheet. It takes an outline and a tab naming its kind, and the **Block** tab shows its settings:

- **Duplicate**, **Remove** and **More**. On a **Map** or **Map + side** sheet, **More** moves a side block with **Move up the stack** or **Move down the stack**.
- **Record**: what the block is about, and whether it follows a parameter, such as *Operator · from the Operator parameter*. **Change** opens the **Params** tab.
- **Title**.
- The block's own settings. A production chart has **Streams** (**Oil**, **Gas**, **Water**), **Show** (**Month**, **Day**, **Cumulative**) and **Scale** (**Log**, **Linear**). Tick **Readers can switch these** to let the people you share with change the view and scale for themselves.
- **Data**: the month the block's data is from, with **Capture data** or **Refresh data** for this block alone.
- On a **Grid** sheet, **Place on the grid**: **Column**, **Row**, **Wide** and **Tall**.

Press <kbd>Esc</kbd> to let go of the block.

Text blocks are written on the **Block** tab. The note under the box shows how to add headings, bold and lists. **Insert a value** puts in a parameter, such as the operator's name, or the **Data month**. It's shaded in the editor and plain to readers, and it changes by itself when the parameter does.

### Keys

| Key | Does |
|---|---|
| <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> (<kbd>Ctrl</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> on Windows) | Undo |
| <kbd>⇧</kbd> <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Z</kbd> or <kbd>⌘</kbd> <kbd>Y</kbd> | Redo |
| <kbd>Esc</kbd> | Let go of the block or mark, or stop drawing |
| <kbd>Delete</kbd> | Remove the mark you picked |

## Maps on a sheet

A map block is drawn like a printed map, with a scale bar, a north arrow and *Data as of Aug 2026 · Source: Texas RRC*. Zoomed out to a county or a basin, it reads like a presentation map: highlighted wells show as coloured dots, and the other wells stay quiet. Zoomed in to a few sections, it draws like a plat from [Map Print Studio](/maps/printing), with RRC well symbols and the land grid.

Select a map to see its settings on the **Block** tab:

- **View** says how the map is framed, such as *Framed on the groups’ wells.* To change it, drag the map on the sheet, and hold <kbd>⌘</kbd> or <kbd>Ctrl</kbd> while you scroll to zoom. Then click **Use this view** to keep it, or **Reset view** to go back.
- **Marks**: see [Marks and planned wells](#marks-and-planned-wells).
- **Basemap**: **Paper**, **Relief**, **Topo**, **Satellite** or **Streets**.
- **Show on map**: **Land grid**, **Counties**, **Leases**, **Wells**, **Laterals**, **Pipelines** and **Contours**.
- **Highlight groups**: sets of wells drawn in their own colour, and counted in the map's legend. **Add group** offers the workbook's groups and a record's wells. **Edit** changes a group's **Name** and **Colour**; a workbook group is changed on the **Params** tab. A map shows up to six.
- **Other wells**, once a map has groups: **Show**, **Dim** or **Hide** the wells outside them.

For a map added from Maps, the **Block** tab may start with *Not drawn on workbook maps yet:* and name the layers on your map that a sheet can't draw yet.

## Marks and planned wells

You can draw on a map once its data is in. Select the map, then click **Draw on the map** under **Marks**. The line under the sheet becomes a row of tools: **Text**, **Arrow**, **Box**, **Area**, **Line**, **Pin**, **Note** and **Planned well**, then **Done drawing**.

Pick a tool and draw; the footer says how. Each tool draws one mark, then the map moves again.

| Tool | How to draw it |
|---|---|
| **Text**, **Pin**, **Note** | Click where it goes. |
| **Arrow** | Drag from its tail to its head, or click one and then the other. |
| **Box** | Drag from one corner to the opposite one, or click one and then the other. |
| **Line** | Click each point, then double-click or press <kbd>Enter</kbd>. |
| **Area** | Click each corner, then double-click or click the first corner. <kbd>Backspace</kbd> takes a corner back. |
| **Planned well** | Click its surface location, then its bottom hole. <kbd>Enter</kbd> keeps it vertical. |

The mark you just drew is picked, and its settings show under **Marks**. Type its words, and give an arrow, box, line or area a **Colour**. Click a mark in the list to pick it again. Drag a picked mark, or one of its handles, to move it. **Remove**, or <kbd>Delete</kbd>, takes it off. <kbd>Esc</kbd> lets go of the mark; press it again, or click **Done drawing**, to stop drawing.

A map holds up to 200 marks. Nothing you draw looks like a well: a pin is a teardrop, a note is a card, and a planned well is a permit symbol (an open circle) with a dashed lateral.

{/* screenshot: workbooks-draw — The Help demo drilling program's map in draw mode: the Draw bar under the sheet, three planned wells and a pin on the map, one planned well picked with Name, Spud, First production, Target and Lateral (ft) under Marks, and the Drilling schedule block beside the map. Annotate: 1 = the Draw bar, 2 = the planned well's settings, 3 = the Drilling schedule block */}

### Planned wells and the schedule

A new planned well is named in order, such as *PLANNED 3H*, and spudded a month after the last one, with first production three months later. Pick it to change its **Name**, **Spud**, **First production**, **Target** and **Lateral (ft)**. Leave the lateral blank to use the length as drawn.

**Import planned wells**, under **Marks**, adds many at once: **From a CSV file…**, or a layer of points or lines **From My Data**. A CSV needs a header row and a latitude and longitude for each surface location, in columns such as `lat` and `lon`. It can also have `bottom_lat` and `bottom_lon`, `name`, `spud`, `first_production`, `target` and `lateral_ft`. Dates such as *2026-11* or *11/2026* both work. Every row left out is listed with the reason. **Frame the map on the planned wells** fits the map to them.

A **Drilling schedule** block lists the planned wells by spud, with **Well**, **Lateral (ft)**, **Spud**, **First production**, and a bar from spud to first production. It follows the wells: move one and its lateral changes; change a date and its bar moves. On its **Block** tab, **Planned wells from** picks the maps it reads (every map readers see, until you pick), and **Months** is **From the wells** or **Set months**.

The **Drilling program** start sets all of this up for an operator and an area.

## Parameters and groups

### Parameters

Parameters are what the workbook is about: an **Operator**, **Lease**, **Field**, **County** or **Well**, the **Frac companies**, an **Area** and a **Month range**. Every block and text that uses one changes with it. The **Params** tab lists them, with how many blocks use each, such as *11 blocks · 3 text*.

To change one, pick another value. The blocks that use it keep their old data under *Out of date.* until you refresh them, and a box under the value says so, such as *9 blocks still show PIONEER data.* Click **Refresh data** to load the new data, or **Undo** to put the old value back. Text you typed with the old name is flagged, so you can fix it.

**Add parameter** adds one the workbook doesn't have yet. **Remove** takes off one that nothing uses.

{/* screenshot: workbooks-params — The Params tab of the Help demo operator review just after the Operator was changed: the changed field outlined, the box saying how many blocks still show the old operator's data with Refresh data and Undo, blocks on the sheet under Out of date., and the footer naming the change. Annotate: 1 = the changed Operator, 2 = Refresh data, 3 = a block marked Out of date. */}

### Groups

Groups are sets of wells or frac jobs. Each is drawn in its own colour on every block that shows it: maps, charts, scorecards and tables. Under **Groups** on the **Params** tab, **Add group** offers:

- each of the workbook's **Frac companies**, as that company's frac jobs;
- a record's wells, such as *Operator’s wells · PIONEER NATURAL RESOURCES USA, INC.*;
- **Frac jobs you filter** or **Wells you filter**.

**Edit** a group to change its **Name**, its **Colour**, and what it **Holds**: **Frac jobs**, **Wells** or **A record’s wells**. For jobs or wells, **Add a filter** narrows them and **Only in the Area** keeps them to the workbook's area. **Done** closes it, and **Remove group** takes it off every block. A workbook holds up to six groups.

**Scorecard**, **Radar chart**, **Distribution** and **Type curve** blocks compare groups of frac jobs.

### Reuse a workbook

At the bottom of the **Params** tab, under **Reuse this workbook**:

- **Make a copy for…** makes a new workbook for other values, such as the same review for another operator. Pick each new value under **In the copy**, beside the one it has **Now**, check **Title of the copy**, and click **Make the copy**. The copy keeps every sheet, loads fresh data, and opens on the first text that still names the old values. That text is flagged *Written for …*, with **Edit** and **Remove**; **Rewrite** asks Ask to rewrite it. The original doesn't change.
- **Save as template** keeps the sheets, layouts and parameters, without the data. Name it and click **Save template**. Your templates are listed under **Templates** on Your pages, and under **Your templates** in **New workbook**.

{/* screenshot: workbooks-copy-for — The Make a copy for… dialog for the Help demo operator review: the Now and In the copy columns with a new operator picked, Title of the copy filled in, the summary of what the copy will do, and Make the copy. Annotate: 1 = the In the copy column, 2 = Make the copy */}

## Keep the data current

A workbook keeps the data it captured. It doesn't update by itself, so what you send today still says the same thing next month. Each block shows its month in its corner, such as *Data as of Aug 2026*.

| A block shows | Because | Do this |
|---|---|---|
| *Data not captured yet* | It's new, or its data hasn't been fetched | Click **Capture data** on it |
| *Out of date.* over its numbers | A parameter or one of its settings changed | Click **Refresh data** on it |
| *This table has 412,000 rows. Narrow it to under 50,000 to keep it.* | A table keeps up to 50,000 rows | **Add a filter**, or **Shorten month range** |

The footer gathers these up. It says *2 blocks have no data yet* with **Capture missing data**, or *Operator changed. 9 blocks are out of date.* with **Refresh data**. When everything is current it reads *All blocks: data as of Aug 2026*, and **Refresh data** there brings every block up to the latest month on file. While data is fetched, the footer counts the blocks, such as *6 of 15 blocks*, and **Cancel** stops it.

A refresh is saved as a new version, so you can go back to the old numbers from [Versions](#versions).

## Ask in the editor

Click **Ask** at the top of the editor. The Ask column opens in place of the panel. Ask about what the workbook shows, or have it changed: *add a sheet with the top 10 leases*, *make this chart linear*, *make this for DEVON*. Ask knows which sheet and block you're on.

Each change Ask makes is saved as a new version, marked *Changed by Ask* in **Versions**, and the editor turns to what changed. Blocks it leaves without data fill in by themselves. **New conversation** starts over, and **Close** brings back the panel. Next time, Ask picks up your last conversation about this workbook.

Asking uses credits, as [Ask on the map](/maps/ask-the-map) does.

## Versions

Click **Versions** at the top. The history opens in place of the panel:

- **Named versions** come first, each with **Restore**.
- Then each day, such as *Today* or *Yesterday*: editing sessions, such as *Dana · 14 edits*, and events, such as *Dana refreshed the data* or *Changed by Ask*.

Click one to see it on the sheet and read what changed. **Restore** makes it the current version again; restoring saves a new version, so nothing is lost. **Name this version**, or **Name the current version** at the top, gives it a name such as *Sent to partner*. **Close** brings back the panel.

{/* screenshot: workbooks-versions — The Versions panel of the Help demo workbook: Name the current version, one named version, today's sessions, and an earlier session selected and shown on the sheet with what it changed, Restore and Name this version. Annotate: 1 = Name the current version, 2 = Restore */}

### When someone else is editing

If someone else saves while you're working, QuickScout combines their changes with yours, sheet by sheet. When you both changed the same sheet, the footer says so, for example *Jane saved 1 minute ago* and *Your edits to sheet 3 overlap theirs.* Choose **Keep theirs**, **Keep mine** or **Save mine as a copy**. Nothing is overwritten until you choose, and the header reads *Your last change isn’t saved yet*.

## Add to a workbook from Maps

With a paid plan, you can send what you're looking at in Maps straight to a workbook.

| From | Click | It adds |
|---|---|---|
| The map | **Add to workbook** on the right rail, under **Print** | A map of your view, with its layers and highlighted wells |
| A lease, field or operator card's production chart | The chart's **More**, then **Add to workbook** | The chart as you have it: its streams, view, scale, and any months you zoomed to |
| The compare tray | **Add to workbook** | A comparison chart of the records in the tray. See [Compare](/maps/compare). |
| An Ask answer | **Add to workbook** under the answer's chart or card | The chart or card, with the question it answered |
| A data table | **Export**, then **Add to workbook** | A table with the tab's filters, columns and order |

A card opens beside the right rail, such as *Add this chart to a workbook*, with a line saying what comes along and what won't. **Recent workbooks** lists the six of yours you edited last. Then:

- **Add as new sheet** puts it on a sheet of its own, at the end of that workbook.
- **More**, beside a workbook, adds it to a sheet you pick instead: **Replace map on sheet…** for a map, **Add to sheet…** for anything else.
- **New workbook…** starts a new workbook and adds it there.

A row at the bottom of the map confirms it for a short while, such as *Added to ‘Midland review’ as Sheet 5*, with **Open workbook**. Nothing on your map changes.

From the map, wells you isolated, a search's matches and Ask's highlights come along as the map's highlight groups, and your sketches and measured lines come along as marks. Some tables can't go to a workbook yet, such as the Search tab and P-4 filings: their **Add to workbook** is greyed out and says why.

Ask can do it for you, too. In [Ask](/maps/ask-the-map), ask it to make or change a workbook, such as *make an operator review of Pioneer*, or to *put this map in my workbook*. It answers with a workbook card: **Open workbook** opens it, and **Edit here** makes it the subject of your next questions.

To go the other way, from a shared workbook back to the live map, see [Open in Maps](/maps/workbooks-share#take-a-shared-workbook-further).

## Limits

- A workbook holds up to 60 sheets, and a sheet up to 12 blocks, two of them maps.
- A workbook holds up to six groups. A map shows up to six highlight groups and 200 marks.
- A table keeps up to 50,000 rows, and shows up to 100 of them on the sheet.
